The rules we hold ourselves to

  • We read the terms first. Before a connector is written, somebody reads that site's robots file and terms and records what they say, with the date, in the code itself. That text goes into the report.
  • We use the official API wherever one exists. Reddit and YouTube are read through their published interfaces, not scraped.
  • We are slow on purpose. Token bucket rate limiting, exponential backoff, and a circuit breaker that stops entirely after repeated refusals. Right now one source is blocking us, and the correct response is to stop asking.
  • We store references and structure, not copies. We keep what we derived and a link back to the original. We are not a mirror of anyone's content.
  • We attribute on every surface. Every review shown on the evidence page carries its source and links out to it.
  • Authors are pseudonymous. We store a stable opaque key so we can spot posting patterns. We do not store names, emails or profile links.
  • If terms forbid it, we drop the source and write down why. The product is built to survive that, which is what makes the rule credible rather than decorative.

What we are not doing

We are not scraping on demand. Nothing is fetched while you use the site; every page you see was computed overnight. We are not reselling anyone's reviews, we are not republishing them in full, and we do not accept payment from any manufacturer or dealer, which is precisely why we can afford to say that a car's service experience is poor.

Why one dead source does not matter much

Each source is an isolated connector with its own failure handling. Losing one degrades coverage and the interface says so, stating how many sources produced each verdict. The design target is that the product stays complete and demonstrable with only three of eight alive. You can see that being tested for real on the admin page right now.
