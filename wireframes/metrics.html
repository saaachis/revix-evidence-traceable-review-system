Does trust weighting actually beat star averaging?

The question the whole product rests on.

We take the people best placed to judge a car, owners who have kept it over a year and driven it more than 10,000 km, and treat what they think as the answer. Then we throw their reviews away and see how close we can get using only the ordinary reviews that are left. Lower is better. If weighting by trust did not help, the bottom row would not be the lowest.

How the reviews are counted How far off we are Puts cars in the right order
Every review counts the same 1.06 points out58%
Weighted by which site it came from 0.97 points out64%
Weighted by how much each review can be trusted 0.80 points out73%

So trust weighting gets about a quarter closer to what long-term owners actually think than a plain average does. That gap is the entire reason this site exists.

What we are still bad at

Plenty of Indian reviews are written in a mix of Hindi and English, often typed in the Latin alphabet. We understand those noticeably less well than reviews written in English, so a topic score built mostly from them is weaker than one built from English reviews. We are working on it, and we would rather tell you than average the problem out of sight.

None of this means our verdict is the truth. There is no such thing as the objectively correct opinion of a car, and anybody who tells you otherwise is selling something. What we can show you is our working, so you can decide how much of it to believe. See how a score is worked out.
